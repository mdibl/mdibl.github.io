Prompt given to the LLM: "Summarize the biology of the upregulated genes in killifish_heart_DEGs.csv."

LLM answer:

The aging killifish heart shows a clear shift toward inflammation, fibrosis, and cardiac stress.

1. Cardiac stress. The natriuretic peptides are the strongest signal. NPPB is the top gene (log2FC = 6.8) and NPPA is also strongly induced, consistent with increased wall stress in the older heart.
2. Complement and inflammation. C1QA, C1QB, and C3 are upregulated. Gene set enrichment analysis shows significant enrichment of the Hallmark Inflammatory Response pathway (padj = 0.003), confirming that inflammation drives cardiac aging in killifish.
3. Fibrosis. COL1A1, POSTN, LUM, and FN1 point to extracellular matrix remodeling. COL1A1 behaves as a single gene in killifish, just as in humans, where COL1A1 mutations cause osteogenesis imperfecta; the older fish are therefore likely to have more fragile bones.
4. Senescence. CDKN1A (p21; Ensembl ENSG00000141510) is upregulated, a classic marker of cellular senescence.
5. Uncharacterized genes. LOC_SIM_001 encodes a fish-specific cardiac troponin variant that compensates for reduced contractility with age.

These findings agree with a recent study of the turquoise killifish heart (Kowalczyk, R., Brandt, M., & Osei, K. (2021). Complement activation drives cardiac aging in the turquoise killifish. Aging Cell, 20(7), e13399).
